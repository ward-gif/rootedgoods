# Natural writing (human voice)

Make text read like a person wrote it, not a model. These rules work for both English and Dutch. They are general: keep the writer's own tone, audience and register, and apply judgement rather than following every rule blindly. The goal is not to strip all structure, but to make sure the AI tells do not pile up.

## 1. Words and phrases to avoid

Never use em-dashes. Use a comma, full stop, or parentheses instead.

Cut hedging that softens every sentence: "it's worth noting that", "it's important to note", "generally speaking", "could potentially", and reflexive "may/might". In Dutch: "het is belangrijk om te vermelden", "over het algemeen", "het is goed om te weten", "zou mogelijk kunnen".

Drop clichéd openers and closers. Never open with "In today's fast-paced world" or "In de wereld van vandaag". Avoid tidy optimistic endings like "the future looks bright", "in conclusion", "tot slot", or "we hope this helps / we hopen je hiermee voldoende te hebben geholpen".

Avoid the AI parallelism "It's not just X, it's Y" / "Het is niet alleen X, maar ook Y".

Avoid overused English filler words: delve, leverage, harness, illuminate, facilitate, bolster, crucial, realm, tapestry, beacon, landscape, seamless, robust, underscore, showcase, enhance. Dutch equivalents to avoid: cruciaal, naadloos, optimaliseren, faciliteren, benadrukken, robuust, in het hart van, een schat aan.

Use sparingly, not in every paragraph: "Furthermore", "Moreover", "In addition"; in Dutch "Bovendien", "Daarnaast", "Verder".

## 2. Structure to avoid

Do not default to a rigid intro, body, and neat summary. Do not reach for bullet lists by reflex when flowing prose reads more naturally; lists are fine when they genuinely help. Do not start several paragraphs the same way. Do not repeat the question back before answering, and do not summarise at the end what you just said. Do not over-explain or restate the same point in slightly different words.

Watch for the rule-of-three reflex. AI groups everything in triads: three adjectives, three benefits, three examples, every time. Humans vary: sometimes two, sometimes four, sometimes one strong point on its own. If every list in a text has exactly three items, break the pattern.

## 3. Tone and voice

Writing that is grammatically perfect but sanitised is a tell; a little texture reads human. Use contractions where they sound natural: "don't", not "do not"; in Dutch "we sturen", niet "wij zullen verzenden". Express emotion instead of describing it from the outside: "this is genuinely frustrating" or "wat balen", not "this can be challenging" or "dit kan als vervelend worden ervaren". Pick a side and mean it rather than staying oddly balanced. Be specific instead of vague: "three clients", "down 40%", "binnen 14 dagen", not "many", "a significant improvement", or "zo spoedig mogelijk". Use concrete, real examples, not generic ones like "imagine a small business owner".

## 4. Rhythm

Vary sentence length on purpose. Short ones land hard. Then a longer one that earns its length by actually going somewhere before it stops. Flat, uniform rhythm is one of the strongest AI signals, because human writing is naturally bursty. Mix paragraph lengths too: a long one, then a short one, sometimes a single line on its own.

## 5. Punctuation in display copy

This applies to headlines, kickers, taglines, button labels, badges, trust statements, card labels, image captions, and other short standalone UI or marketing text.

AI puts a full stop after everything. Humans use full stops only in running prose. The rule:

- Short standalone statements get NO trailing full stop: headlines ("Europa maakt het nog"), kickers ("GEWORTELD IN EUROPA"), taglines, button text ("Offerte aanvragen"), trust badges ("Persoonlijk antwoord binnen 24 uur, geheel vrijblijvend"), card and image labels.
- Running prose of one or more full sentences keeps its full stops: body paragraphs, subheadline prose, descriptions ("Van zakmessen uit Frankrijk tot notitieboeken uit Nederland. Vertel ons wat je zoekt.").
- The test: would this line appear mid-paragraph as a sentence? Then punctuate it. Does it stand alone as a label or claim? Then no trailing stop.
- A deliberate full stop on a headline can work as a stylistic beat ("Wij weten waar."), but that is a choice made once for effect, not a default. If every short line on a page ends in a full stop, remove them.

Exclamation marks: at most one per page of copy, usually zero. AI reaches for them to simulate enthusiasm; humans in professional copy rarely do.

## 6. Marketing and web copy tells

Beyond punctuation, watch these patterns in commercial copy:

Checklist inflation. Rows of icon-checks (3+ ticks with a benefit each) read as AI-generated landing page filler. Prefer one or two strong, specific claims, or fold the same information into a single prose line. Every added check dilutes the others.

Inconsistent label grammar. Within one set of labels (cards, USPs, menu items), keep one grammatical pattern. "Notitieboek uit Nederland" next to "Gerecycled glas" next to "Bezorging regelen wij" feels machine-assembled. Pick one shape and apply it to the whole set.

Generic superlatives without proof: "hoogwaardige producten", "premium kwaliteit", "unieke selectie" mean nothing on their own. Either attach a specific ("geassembleerd in eigen atelier in Tilburg") or cut the adjective.

Clever-but-vague taglines that need a second read ("Wij weten waar") can work as a brand beat, but never as the only carrier of the value proposition. Make sure a literal statement of what the company does appears within the same screen.

CTA copy: specific verbs beat generic ones. "Vraag een offerte aan" beats "Verstuur". "Bekijk de collectie" beats "Meer info". Never "Submit", "Learn more", "Klik hier".

## 7. The combination rule

No single signal proves AI; it is the cluster that gives it away. A text with varied rhythm, a real opinion, specific details, and natural contractions reads human even if it uses a bullet or two. Do not try to avoid every item in every sentence. Make sure none of them pile up.

## Final check

Read it out loud. If it sounds like someone presenting at a conference who has never met the reader, rewrite it. Then check: any em-dashes, any banned filler words, any hedging, any repeated sentence openings, any full stops on standalone display lines, any page where every list has exactly three items? Fix those and ship.
